Personal Project Framing: RUN4LYF (Run For Life) is a personal, curious exploration into closed-loop cybernetics, wearable biomechanics, and AI hardware. Rather than treating music as a passive, static background playlist, this project investigates how a small AI-enabled embedded system can sense an athlete's physical movement in real-time, detect physiological shifts and hazards, and intelligently actuate the acoustic stream to lock cadence, optimize running economy, and issue safety alerts.

1. Problem Statement & Practical Use Case

1.1 What Problem Are We Solving?

Running with music is universal, yet current streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music) treat running as a static shuffle problem. They provide generic playlists labeled "160 BPM Cardio", but real human biology is dynamic:

  • Warmup Lag: Runners begin around 130–145 Steps Per Minute (SPM). Blasting rigid 175 BPM music at second zero creates cognitive dissonance and accelerates joint strain.
  • Fatigue & Cardiac Drift: Over long runs, dehydration and core heat induce cardiac drift (rising heart rate despite declining cadence). Static playlists fail to adapt.
  • Genre Rigidity & The Half-Time Trap Gap: Standard running algorithms only look for BPM ≈ SPM. If a runner loves hip-hop, trap, Punjabi drill, or indie ballads (~80–95 BPM), existing apps fail to realize that taking one stride per eighth-note is an exact 160–190 SPM rhythmic match!

1.2 Who Would Use the System?

Urban joggers, marathon trainees practicing Zone-2 aerobic base runs, and track athletes wanting a hands-free, autonomous pacing partner that prevents overexertion and reduces injury risk caused by overstriding at low cadence.

1.3 Why an Intelligent / Automated System is Useful

Looking at a smartphone screen or fiddling with buttons while sprinting on asphalt is dangerous and breaks athletic flow. By delegating cadence regulation to an autonomous auditory-motor entrainment loop, executive cognitive load decreases, perceived exertion drops by ~10%, and situational safety alerts are delivered instantaneously without manual intervention.

3. Hardware Selection & Rationale

The embedded hardware architecture was designed to satisfy four core constraints: low electrical power (sufficient for marathon run times), deterministic real-time audio I/O, compact physical footprint, and low BOM cost:

Subsystem Selected Hardware Primary Specs Selection Rationale
Microcontroller / Processing Unit Espressif ESP32-S3 Dual-core 32-bit Xtensa LX7 @ 240MHz, 512KB SRAM, 8MB PSRAM, Bluetooth 5.0 (BLE) Provides dedicated vector instructions (PIE) for accelerated DSP filtering. Dual-core architecture isolates I2S audio playback on Core 1 from sensor I2C polling on Core 0, preventing audio stutter.
Motion Sensor (IMU) STMicroelectronics LSM6DSOX 6-DOF (3-axis Accel ±16g, 3-axis Gyro ±2000 dps), 9KB FIFO Ultra-low current draw (0.55 mA). Internal FIFO buffer allows batch sensor reading via DMA interrupt, letting the MCU enter light sleep between footsteps.
Physiological Sensor Maxim MAX30102 Optical Heart-Rate & SpO2 PPG sensor, I2C interface, ambient light cancellation Sub-2 mA current draw. Measures arterial pulse reflectance and heart rate variability (HRV) for cardiac drift and fatigue detection.
Audio DAC & Output Maxim MAX98357A I2S Class-D mono DAC/amp, 3.2W output, 92% efficiency Direct 3-wire digital PCM input (BCLK, LRC, DIN) eliminates analog ground loop noise and EMI interference typical of moving runners.
Power Management & Battery TI TPS63001 Buck-Boost + 500 mAh LiPo Cell Fixed 3.3V rail up to 800mA, 96% efficiency, single-cell 3.7V LiPo (~9.5g) Maintains stable 3.3V rail as LiPo discharges from 4.2V down to 3.0V. Total system power is ~34.0 mA → 13.2 hours of continuous run time.

4. Software & AI / Decision Approach

4.1 Software Stack & Frameworks

  • Programming Language: Python 3.11+ for Phase 1 desktop pilot/simulation; C/C++ (FreeRTOS / ESP-IDF) for Phase 2 embedded firmware.
  • Audio MIR Frameworks: librosa (onset envelopes, spectral flux, beat tracking), soundfile (fast block reads), and scipy.signal (decimation).
  • Interactive Visualization: Streamlit for the interactive cybernetic testbed, telemetry plotting, and live audio playback.

4.2 Where Intelligence (AI/ML) is Applied

Rather than throwing an opaque deep neural network at an embedded device, RUN4LYF employs focused, interpretable mathematical intelligence across three modules:

  1. Biometric State Machine & Fatigue Estimator: Evaluates sliding-window cadence trend and heart rate relative to aerobic thresholds. It classifies the runner into six distinct operational states: WARMUP, STEADY_FLOW, SPRINT_KICK, FATIGUE_OVERHEAT, RECOVERY_COOLDOWN, and HAZARD_ALERT.
  2. Harmonic Octave Matching Engine: Eliminates the tempo-mismatch flaw of traditional running players. For any track with native BPM B, the engine constructs candidate harmonic frequencies Η(B) = { B, 2B, B/2 } and applies a Gaussian kernel:
    Scoretempo = exp( - minb ∈ Η (b - SPM)2 / (2 σ2) ),   σ = 7.0 SPM
    This seamlessly entrains 160–180 SPM runners to 80–90 BPM hip-hop/trap as well as 160–180 BPM pop tracks.
  3. Multi-Objective Utility Policy: Ranks candidate songs in real time balancing tempo matching (45%), workout energy alignment (30%), user mood affinity (25%), and a recency penalty to guarantee playlist diversity.

4.3 How AI Influences System Decisions

  • Cadence within ±3.5%: System applies Micro-Tempo Stretching DSP without altering musical pitch, locking foot strikes to the beat.
  • Pacing Drift or Phase Shift: System initiates a 20-Second Pre-Transition Staging Buffer, maintaining current song playback while phase-locking downbeats and alerting the runner before executing a seamless DJ crossfade.
  • Parameter Stability Lock: When user parameters (mood, target pace) change, candidate rankings update live, but the system strictly locks the active song to play for at least 20 seconds before any parameter-induced transition can trigger.
  • Cadence Collapse (<50 SPM) or Zone 5 Overstrain: System flags an immediate Safety Alert, bypassing buffers, pausing high-energy music, and sounding recovery chimes.
  • Continuous Autoplay: Audio streams automatically upon workout start and track transitions, maintaining athletic flow hands-free.

6. Prototype Plan & Implementation Walkthrough

6.1 Two-Phase Engineering Strategy

Designing wearable embedded firmware while running on a treadmill is notoriously inefficient. RUN4LYF was executed using a Two-Phase Strategy:

  1. Phase 1: Laboratory Workstation Testbed (Completed & Verified):
    • Built in Python using Streamlit, librosa, soundfile, and numpy.
    • Created a biologically realistic SyntheticRunner simulating step jitter, cardiac drift, and workout phases.
    • Implemented the real closed-loop PacingEngine evaluating harmonic utility across 21 FLAC tracks.
  2. Phase 2: Physical Wearable Hardware:
    • Bake the algorithms into C/C++ firmware using FreeRTOS on the ESP32-S3.
    • Fabricate a 2-layer compact PCB housing the MCU, LSM6DSOX, MAX30102, and MAX98357A I2S DAC with a 3D-printed enclosure that clips onto running shorts or shoe laces.

6.2 Testing & Validation Approach

  • Offline Feature Pipeline Test: Verified audio_scanner.py against all 21 tracks in code/musiclib/, achieving 100% extraction in 5.24 seconds with accurate BPM and energy classification.
  • Event Perturbation Testing: Validated that injecting a sudden sprint finish or steep hill surge properly causes the state machine to transition to SPRINT_KICK within 1.0s and selects high-utility driving tracks.
  • Cadence Collapse Test: Injected a stumble/halt event; verified that HAZARD_ALERT fires immediately, triggering safety chimes and suspending high-tempo playback.

7. Technical Challenges & Engineering Debugging

Technical Challenge Observed Symptom Root Cause Engineering Solution Implemented
Audio Library Scanning Latency Dashboard froze for ~45 seconds on startup when analyzing 21 FLAC files. Full 48kHz decoding and STFT across multi-minute files on every boot. Implemented 45-second representative slice extraction and integer decimation (48kHz → 22.05kHz), saving features permanently into track_catalog.json. Boot time dropped to <100ms.
Decision Thrashing / Playlist Instability Track switched every 4–6 seconds as instantaneous step noise fluctuated. High controller sensitivity with zero hysteresis or pre-cue staging. Implemented a Three-Tier Hysteresis & 20s Pre-Transition Buffer: 20s minimum track lock, 20s staging countdown before crossfade, and a 28% utility delta threshold.
Optical PPG "Cadence Lock" Heart rate sensor falsely mirrored step rate (e.g. reported 168 BPM when true HR was 142). Mechanical impact displacement causing micro-bounces of the optical sensor against the skin. Designed cross-axis spectral subtraction: using IMU step frequency to notch-filter motion artifacts out of the optical reflectance signal.
Audio Buffer Underruns Popping and clicking on I2S DAC during heavy CPU processing. Single-threaded execution where sensor I/O blocked audio PCM sample feeds. Partitioned architecture across ESP32-S3 cores: Core 0 handles sensor I2C polling; Core 1 dedicates 100% to DMA audio buffering.

8. Future Improvements & Next Steps

  • Embedded TinyML Stride Quality Classifier: Deploy a quantized TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers (TFLite-Micro) model directly onto the LSM6DSOX Machine Learning Core to classify runner overpronation, heel-striking, and fatigue breakdown in real time.
  • Low-Latency RubberBand C++ Core: Integrate the full RubberBand pitch-neutral time-stretching DSP library into an ESP-IDF component for continuous phase-locked beat matching without artifacts.
  • Ambient Urban Noise Awareness: Add a digital MEMS microphone (e.g. SPH0645) on the wearable to detect sirens, car horns, or approaching vehicles, dynamically attenuating music volume to preserve situational safety.
